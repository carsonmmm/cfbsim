# Football Empire patch notes

## 10.04 — Two-way players, rebuilt
- Player Career: playing two positions is now something you manage every week, not a switch you flip once.
- New: a weekly two-way plan on the Home page. Choose mostly your main position, balanced, more of the second position, or every snap. Each choice shows the snaps you get on the other side and what it does to your fatigue.
- New: fatigue. Playing both ways wears you down. Tired legs cost rating points on both sides and raise your injury risk. A lighter plan, a bye week or a rest week brings it back, and the trainer will step in when it gets bad.
- New: you split your own training. Pick how much of every skill point goes to the second position (none, 25%, 50% or 75%) and spend those points on its ratings yourself. The second position no longer grows much on its own.
- New: the coach has to trust you over there. You start as a situational player, then earn a place in the rotation, then a starting job. Good games on that side raise his trust; playing worn down lowers it.
- New: talks with the coach. He may ask you to give up the second position when your main one slips, or ask a one-position player to help out when a starter is hurt. Once a season you can also ask him to try you on the other side, so a career no longer has to start as two-way.
- New: schools differ. Some promise both ways, some are open to it, some want one position. The recruiting and transfer pages show which is which, and it sets how much the coaches trust you on day one. Two-way players get a little more NIL money.
- New: awards and records. A national Two-Way Player of the Year, an All-State two-way honor in high school, a lift in the Heisman race for doing both well, and a news story when you score a touchdown and get a takeaway or sack in the same game. Your player page keeps a count of two-way games, iron-man games and those big games.
- New: the draft and the NFL. Before the draft you see how many clubs grade you at each position and how many would use you at both. The club that takes you may ask you to switch positions, or give you a package of snaps on the other side. Without one, you can ask for a package once a season. The club takes it away if your main job slips.
- You can also make your second position your main one when it has caught up.
- Careers from before this update keep their second position and pick up the new system where they are.

## 10.03 — Promote right from the open job
- Easier to find (Athletic Director and Coach): when you fire a coordinator, the open job on the Staff page now lists your position coaches right on its card. Tap a name to promote him. Each button shows the rating he would have in the new job. Before, the choices were only in a separate card further down the page.
- The same goes for an open special teams job, and for the head coach job: in the offseason you promote a coordinator from the card, and during the season you name him interim.

## 10.02 — Promote from within
- New (Athletic Director and Coach): promote your own coaches. When a job is open, the Staff page now has a "Promotions and interims" card. A position coach can move up to coordinator on his side of the ball, any position coach can take special teams, and a coordinator can become head coach. He gets a raise and a new contract, and his old job opens up.
- New: an interim at every job. Name one of your coaches as the interim and he keeps his own job while he covers the open one. A graduate assistant can hold a coordinator or position job too. When you are ready, give the interim the job, end the interim, or hire someone else.
- New: choose your interim head coach. If you fire the head coach during the season, you pick which coordinator takes over, and you can change your mind. When the season ends he stays until you decide: give him the job for good, promote a different coordinator, or hire from outside. The offseason waits for that decision.
- New: an assistant head coach. Give the title to any one assistant. It comes with a 15% raise, he is first in line if the head coach job opens, and he loses less in the bigger job. It is optional: you never have to fill it.
- Coach mode: a head coach can do all of this for the jobs under him, inside his assistant pool. Nobody can be promoted into your own job.
- A coach in a bigger job starts a little below his old rating while he learns it. Autopilot settles any interims for you in the offseason.

## 10.01 — The admin panel keeps what you type
- Fixed: in the admin panel, only the first thing you typed was saved. After each change the page redrew and the cursor left the form, so the next field you moved to lost what you typed, and numbers typed after that acted as keyboard shortcuts and could even switch pages. Fields now save in place: move between them with Tab or the mouse and every value is kept.
- Fixed (Coach mode): changes to your own coach did not last. The panel was editing a copy of you that the game rebuilds from your career. Your name, skills, salary and years now change the coach himself, and stay changed.
- New (Coach mode): your own card in the admin panel also lets you set your reputation, job security, age and, as head coach, your assistant pool.
- Fixed: another school's overall rating, set in the admin panel, drifted back to where it started after a season or two. The change now sticks.
- The same fix applies to the admin page in Player Career.
- Some values are meant to move after you set them: injuries and suspensions count down each week, the meters and the bank balance change as you play, and a school's style and star players are redrawn every offseason.

## 10.00 — A New Beginning
- Version 10. Since 9.54 the game has gained two new modes (Agent and High School Coach), a nemesis who follows you for a whole career, scandals you can cause, player stories, conferences that raid each other, travel and altitude, the alumni network, season recaps and career pictures, class files, and a rebuilt Coach mode. The notes for 9.55 to 9.62 below have the details.
- New and optional: the real 2026 pack. When you start an athletic director or coach career at a college, switch on "Real 2026 rosters and schedule". It is off unless you turn it on, and it only affects the career you are starting.
- Your school gets its real 2026 roster: names, positions, classes, numbers, sizes and hometowns, for all 138 schools. Each real player takes the rating of a generated player at his position, so your team is exactly as strong as it would have been. Ratings are the game's own estimates, not real evaluations.
- The 2026 regular season is the real schedule for every school, including neutral-site games and opponents from outside the top division. A handful of late-August and December games are played in an open week. From 2027 the game builds the schedule as it always has.
- The star players the game names at other schools are real players from those schools.
- Real players never get negative traits or off-field storylines, so decisions about injuries and scandals do not involve them. Michigan keeps the hand-rated real roster it already had.
- The pack is a separate download of about 260 KB, fetched only when you switch it on. Old saves are not changed.

## 9.62 — A coach's career
- Coach mode, fixed: as head coach, hiring an assistant could fail with no message. The cause was that candidates asked for big-program money at every school, so at smaller schools nobody fit your assistant pool. Candidates now ask for money on your school's scale. This also makes assistants cheaper for athletic directors at smaller schools.
- Every offer that does not go through now tells you why: he declined, he is over your pool (and by how much), the job is not open, or it is not yours to fill. Candidates you cannot afford are marked before you ask, and you can still talk them down with Negotiate.
- New: widen the search. When nobody left on a list will take the job at your price, bring in three more candidates, once a week for each job. One of them is always a young coach who wants the chance.
- NFL head coaches can now change their own staff from the Staff page: let an assistant go, and offer the job to a candidate. Before, there was no way to do it.
- New: promotions during the season. A head coach on a hot seat, or a coordinator whose unit is near the bottom of the country, can be fired after a loss. If you are next in line you can be asked to take over as interim, running the team or the unit for the rest of the season: a coordinator for the head job, a position coach for his coordinator's. It does not happen in your first season at a school, and an interim cannot change the staff.
- After the season the school decides. Win as the interim and it offers you the job for good; otherwise it hires someone else and you return to your old job, with the season on your record.
- Fixed: taking the head coaching job at your own school left you in your old job with the new salary. It is now a real promotion, and it is marked as one on the job market.
- The job market: a position coach now sometimes sees the coordinator job above him open up; every opening says why it is open and how the team did; once each offseason you can wait for a second wave of about nine more openings and two more interviews; and your school may answer a better offer with a new deal.
- Fixed: a coach who was fired or not kept, with no offers left and no interviews left, could not move on. A smaller job now always calls.
- Old saves load as before.

## 9.61 — Friday nights
- New: High School Coach, on the Home screen. Take over a small-town team: about forty kids, four years each, ten games and the playoffs.
- Develop them. Players improve with practice, faster if they work hard, and fastest if you spend your own time on them. Set how the team practices and what offense it runs each preseason.
- Decisions on and off the field: a star who is failing algebra, a booster's son who is not playing, a private school after your best junior, and a college scout with time to watch one senior.
- Signing day: your seniors sign with colleges, from two-star to five-star. With Shared world on, they are on the recruiting board of the same year in your athletic director careers on this device.
- Build the program with the booster fund (weight room, youth program, film room), win state titles, and earn seven milestones.
- Move up. Bigger high schools call when you have earned it, and in time a college does. Taking the college job starts a Coach career there with a reputation head start. Three save slots.
- The Home screen is back to four cards across, now that there are eight ways to play.

## 9.60 — Agent mode
- New: Agent mode, on the Home screen. Run a sports agency. You earn 3% of every contract your clients sign, every year it runs.
- Signing season: draft prospects are choosing agents. Each wants something different from his career: the biggest contract, a club that wins, a starting job, or to play near home. Find out what, pitch it, and he may choose you over the big agencies.
- The draft: decide how each rookie prepares. Where he is taken sets his first contract, and your fee, for four years.
- Free agency: clubs make offers and you negotiate. Asking for more can raise an offer or end the talk; more bidders give you more leverage. A deal that misses what your client wanted costs you his trust, and a client who stops trusting you fires you.
- The season: holdouts, trade requests, endorsements, bad headlines, and rival agencies taking your stars to dinner.
- Build the agency: hire scouting, negotiation and marketing staff, earn a reputation, and chase seven milestones. Three save slots.
- Agent mode is a game of its own with a lighter league behind it. It does not share players with your other careers yet.

## 9.59 — Bring your own class
- New: class files. The Save file page can save the current recruiting class (college) or draft class (NFL) as a spreadsheet file, and load one back. Edit a class, build your own, or swap one with a friend. Add players to the class or replace it; recruits who have already committed stay.
- A class file is one player a line: name, position, then stars, rating, potential and hometown for recruits, or age, rating, potential and college for draft prospects.
- Old saves load as before.

## 9.58 — Every player has a story
- New: player stories. Every player page now ends with his story: how he was signed and who else wanted him, each season, his awards, his injuries, and for former players how he left and what became of him. Open a former player from the History page, where a new card lists the ones people still talk about.
- Stories carry between careers on this device. Players an athletic director sends to the NFL Draft can turn up in the same year's draft class in a general manager career (Shared world on), and their page there tells the college half: that you signed them, where, and what they won.
- Realignment has teeth. Strong conferences now take schools from weaker power conferences, and the league that was raided fills the hole from below. The Standings page has a table of every conference by strength, with who moved and when.
- A conference may invite your school (athletic director). The offer lays out the TV money, the level of play, where you would rank, the travel and the exit fee. Accept, ask them to cover the fee and risk losing the offer, or decline. If your rival stays behind, some fans will not forgive it.
- Old saves load as before. Players signed before this update have a shorter first chapter.

## 9.57 — Someone who remembers
- New: your nemesis (athletic directors and general managers). One of your rival front offices is now a person who lasts your whole career, with the same name and style wherever you go. If you change jobs, even from college to the NFL, he turns up at a rival of the new one.
- He remembers. The Rivals page shows your head-to-head record and everything that has happened between you, and what he says to the press after you play depends on it.
- He makes moves aimed at you. In the offseason he may offer your best coordinator a job: match it, go after his staff instead, or let him go. In college he may go after a recruit who is leaning your way: answer with money, send the head coach, or let it go. A move you do not answer goes his way.
- New: scandals you can cause (college athletic director). Three temptations can come up: a man with an envelope for a top recruit, a star's failed test days before a big game, and an injury kept off the report. Each gives you something now and raises your NCAA risk.
- A secret stays live for three seasons. If the NCAA finds a violation while one is live, the story names it and you choose how to answer: take responsibility, fire a coordinator and blame him, or fight it in public. The History page lists what only you know and what came out.
- The newer decisions (injuries and scandals) come up more often than in 9.56, where they were too rare.
- Fixed: an Ironman athletic director who moved to the NFL lost Ironman. It now carries over.
- Scandals only involve fictional players. Old saves load as before, and a nemesis appears in them from the next week you play.

## 9.56 — The road, the training room and the ones who left
- Travel matters (Updated balance): a team that crosses time zones to play gives up a little, more for each zone, and so does a visitor playing a mile up at places like Air Force, Wyoming, Colorado, Utah, BYU or Denver. The game preview tells you when it applies. It is a small edge, about half a home-field advantage at most. Classic saves are unchanged.
- Athletic director: two new decisions about injured players. When the doctors advise a player to stop, you can support a medical retirement, leave it to his family, or tell him the team needs him. And before a rivalry or top-10 game, a hurt starter may ask to play: let him, and about two times in five he makes it worse.
- A player who comes back against medical advice is slower and gets hurt more often.
- New: the alumni network, on the History page of college careers. Former players in the NFL give money back each offseason, recruits at a star's old position are easier to land, and now and then a former player applies to coach for you, for less than the going rate.
- These decisions only involve fictional players. Old saves load as before.

## 9.55 — Look back on it
- NFL seasons now get a recap, like college seasons: a season grade, the best win, the toughest loss, the comeback of the year and your top players. Open it from the History page. Seasons played before this update have no recap.
- Every recap lists the decisions you made that season, and has a button to save or share a picture of that season in your team's colors.
- New: the career timeline. One tall picture of your whole career, season by season, to save or post. It is on the History page, and on the Stats page in Player Career.
- New: feedback. The Home screen and Settings have buttons to report a problem or suggest an idea. They open a short form with your game version, mode and device already filled in.
- Old saves load as before.

## 9.54 — Ironman everywhere

- Easier to read: the Inbox count and the green and red labels now have stronger contrast in the themes where they were faint. Every theme is checked in light and dark.
- Leaderboards, posting scores, cloud saves and the friends league are switched off for now. They will come back when the site has its own sign-in. Everything else, including the daily challenge and challenge codes, works as before.
- collegefbsim.com now counts visits and which modes get opened, with no cookies and nothing that identifies you. It is noted on the Save file page.
- Old saves load as before.

## 9.53 — A new front door
- Home screen: the career you played last is at the top with one Continue button. New players get three quick picks to start with. The scenarios fold away until you want them.
- NFL General Manager has its own card on the Home screen. It was always in the game, behind the Athletic Director card.
- New: a fantasy draft start for general managers. Every player in the league goes into one pool and all 32 clubs draft in a snake order until each roster is full. Make your own picks or let the club draft for you. Players keep their contracts, and clubs stay under the cap.

- Install the game: on collegefbsim.com you can add Football Empire to your home screen, and it opens with no connection once you have played it online.
- The Save file page shows how much of the browser's storage your saves use, and the game asks the browser to keep them.
- On collegefbsim.com the friends league and the leaderboard prompts are hidden, since they only work where the game is shared with sign-in.
- Old saves load as before.

## 9.52 — Everyone wants the No. 1 recruit
- Player Career: fixed a recruit who was far better than every school getting no offers at all, even as the No. 1 player in the nation. The best players are now a perfect fit for the best programs, with long-shot offers from smaller schools close to home.
- Player Career: the "nobody reaches signing day without a place to play" guarantee now works. A senior with fewer than three offers gets them at the end of his last season.
- Player Career: a school that pulled its offer can come back the next season if you play your way back into its range.
- Player Career: the transfer portal no longer passes over a transfer for being too good.
- Saves that are sitting with no offers get them as soon as they load.

## 9.51 — Straight to it
- Every mode: the Inbox takes you there. With one thing waiting, the Inbox button goes straight to the page and the card that needs you. With more, each item (or its Go button) does, and the card you land on lights up for a moment. Decisions you have to answer are listed first, in bold.
- The Inbox and the "Needs your attention" card now share one list, so they always agree.
- Every mode: decisions you have to answer (weekly decisions, press conferences, injuries and rehab plans, choosing your college, the commissioner's desk and agenda, the owner's moments) move to the top of the page, above the getting-started checklist and everything else. On every other page, a banner at the top says what is waiting and takes you to it.

## 9.50 — Easier to find your way
- Every mode: the tab bar shows the sections you use most, and everything else is one tap away under More (with the sidebar, under More sections). Prefer every tab at once? Settings, Tabs: All.
- Every mode: a "Needs your attention" card at the top of the Office (or Home in Player Career) lists what is waiting for you elsewhere, like decisions, offseason steps, injured starters in the lineup, the cap, trade offers, sponsor offers or votes, with a button to go there.
- Every mode: a guided first season. New careers get a short "Getting started" checklist on the home page that ticks itself off as you go. Hide it anytime.
- On phones: long tables (rosters, lists of owners and more) show the first ten rows with a "Show all" button.
- One set of words and numbers: the glossary now explains reputation, legacy, job security, brand, club value and how money is shown. Big money reads the same in Owner and Commissioner modes, and the Commissioner's tab is called Legacy, like the Owner's.

## 9.49 — The owner's world
- Owner mode: hire a president of business operations to set prices, sign sponsors and run ticketing for you, or keep doing it yourself. Good presidents earn more; their style (revenue first, fans first, premium, cost cutter) shows.
- Sponsors: seven kinds of deals (stadium club, beer, bank, airline, technology, sportsbook and, once the owners allow it, a jersey patch). Sign, pass, or ask for 15% more and risk them walking. A club you buy comes with some deals already in place.
- Local TV and radio: sign with a local network, or start the club's own channel (it costs money up front and pays more after a few years).
- Tickets: a season-ticket discount, premium seat prices, a family section and a student section, plus a fan survey every season on value, game day and the team. The Business tab now has charts of the club's value and where the money comes from and goes.
- The stadium: a three-phase stadium district (plaza and restaurants, a hotel and concert hall, offices and apartments), stadium features (grass or turf, a fan plaza, a sportsbook lounge, a giant video board), international home games abroad, and rare storms or power failures that move home games and cost millions. Insurance is on by default.
- The other owners: 31 owners with names and styles (big spenders, penny-pinchers, old guard, newcomers, mavericks). Votes are counted owner by owner; they remember how you voted, who did them a favor and who insulted them. Court them over dinner. New agenda items: jersey patches, private equity, more international games, and league expansion where every owner gets a share of the fees.
- Awards: Coach, Executive and Owner of the Year every season.
- Money and people: sell or buy back 5% of the club, bring in a private-equity fund (once the league allows it), negotiate salaries with the GM, head coach and president you hire, and decide on extensions when their contracts run out.
- Owner moments: sit down with an unhappy player, watch practice, visit a hospital, throw a draft party, and induct retired greats into the Ring of Honor (four moments a season).
- Legacy: a franchise history page (banners, season by season, owners, the Ring of Honor and a club museum), 14 owner achievements, and a ranking of every owner by legacy. New owners get a guided first season on the Owner's suite.
- Removed from Owner mode only (the other modes keep them): Pick'em, the friends league, calling plays and the two-minute drill in live games (owners watch), camp battles and growth goals, the rival front offices page, the league-rule settings (the owners vote on rules now), the GM tutorial, the expansion uniform-style menu (it did nothing) and the sportsbook moment (sportsbooks are a sponsor offer now). The video board moved from stadium improvements to stadium features.
- Owner saves from v9.48 keep working; the new systems start the next time you play them.

## 9.48 — Owner mode
- New: Owner mode (Mode 5 on the Home screen). Buy an NFL club or start an expansion club, and run it from the owner's suite. It uses one of your five AD save slots.
- Getting a club: four clubs are for sale, each with a story. Put together the money from your own fortune, club debt (up to the league limit) and minority partners; the controlling owner must put in at least 30% of the equity. Outbid the other groups, then win at least 24 of the owners' votes.
- Expansion: pick a city (market, local wealth, football passion, stadium), choose a conference and division, make up a name, colors and a crest, pay the fee, and stock the roster in an expansion draft against the other new club. New clubs get 75% of the national money for three seasons and often start in a rented stadium.
- Three scenario starts with goals: the worst club in the smallest market, football's new frontier (an expansion city that never had the NFL), and the family business (51% of a club, an old stadium and a lot of debt). A former player or GM from another career on this device can buy in as a 10% partner and take control after a season.
- The business: a full profit-and-loss statement (national money, tickets, suites, concessions, parking, sponsors, naming rights, merchandise, stadium events, revenue sharing; payroll, staff, football budgets, stadium costs, interest). Set ticket and concession prices, decide how much profit to pay out, borrow or pay down debt, and watch the club's value. Run out of money and the league forces a sale.
- The stadium: seven improvement projects, naming-rights offers, and a new stadium (size, open air, dome or retractable roof) financed with public money, seat licenses, a league loan and your own money. The city may say yes, send it to the voters, or turn you down. Threaten to leave, or ask the owners to approve a move to a new city.
- Fans and brand: fan mood, brand, city relations, season tickets and a waiting list, community programs, new uniforms (fans may hate them), throwback games, and moments that ask for the owner's answer.
- The football side: hire a GM or be your own GM (switch each offseason). Hire and fire the GM and head coach, set a direction (win now, balanced, rebuild) and five football budgets. Orders to your GM (release, trade, re-sign, extend, free-agent and draft targets, blocking his deadline deal) count as meddling, and good candidates turn down meddling owners.
- League politics: an owners' meeting every spring with rule changes that really change the league, revenue sharing, other clubs' sales and moves, your own proposal, Super Bowl and draft hosting bids, favors you can call in, labor talks that can end in a lockout, TV deals, and a relationship with the commissioner (prime-time games, or fines).
- Legacy: points for winning, titles, a growing club, a new stadium and community work; the Hall of Fame for owners; sell the club, hand it to an heir and keep playing, or retire.
- Balance (NFL, Updated balance): your own club no longer wins far more than its roster should. Game plans used to help only your side; they count for less now, since the other club game-plans too. Locker-room morale moves strength half as much, and your games now play to their own pregame odds. Tested on autopilot, a GM's club averaged 12.8 wins and won the Super Bowl a third of the time; now it averages about 8.4 wins, in line with its roster. This also applies to NFL general managers and NFL coaches. Classic saves are unchanged.
- Fixed: in a league with more or fewer than 32 clubs (commissioner expansion), some clubs got almost all home games one season and none the next. Home and away games are balanced now.

## 9.47 — Harder climbs
- Coach (Updated balance): bigger jobs want a track record. Jumping more than one rung, or to a much more prestigious school, now takes several years in your current role, and an NFL head job wants NFL coordinator experience first. One hot season no longer lands a Power 4 head job; a sustained run still opens doors.
- Coach (Updated balance): bosses run out of patience sooner, so losing seasons can get you fired. The office hint shows the line.
- Difficulty now matters more for coaches: Hard makes every promotion harder to earn, Easy makes them a bit easier.
- Player Career (Updated growth): making an NFL roster is no longer automatic. Late-round picks, undrafted players and minimum-deal signings can be cut in camp and land on the practice squad ($13,000 a week), with a chance each week to be promoted.
- Player Career (Updated growth): free agency is pickier, especially for older players and backups who barely played, aging hits harder after your peak, and low-rated backups get released more often.
- Player Career (Updated growth): your potential ceiling is lower and set partly by difficulty (higher on Easy, lower on Hard), and growing past it is much slower. Pro Bowls and season awards take a better season, more so on Hard.
- Careers on Generous growth and saves on the Classic balance play exactly as before.
- Fixed: the Front office tab could fail to draw if a GM save was opened on it.

## 9.46 — Finishing touches
- Sim options: "Sim to…" can now stop on its own when your job gets shaky or a promise comes due (on by default), and when a starter is hurt for two weeks or more or a top commit flips (switch those on in the sim menu).
- Recruiting: compare up to three recruits side by side (⇄ on the board): ratings as your scouts read them, scheme fit, where he'd slot on your depth chart today, whether he'd likely redshirt, how badly you need the position, his interest and any promise you made.
- Player Career: the Player page has one money plan: what's in the bank, this year's money in and out, career earnings, your salary or NIL after your agent's cut, guaranteed money left on your deal, and what each training program costs.
- Coach (Updated balance): your week has about 30 working hours. Road trips, home visits, player meetings, install walkthroughs and game-plan packages all come out of it; your staff handles what you don't get to. The office shows how much of the week is left.
- Commissioner: competitive balance on the League health card is now measured on each level's own scale (NFL rosters sit much closer together than college ones).
- Fixed: in Player Career, the decision journal (v9.31) shared a place in the save with retired jersey numbers, which could mix them up after you retire. The journal now has its own place; one saved the old way moves over on its own.

## 9.45 — A shared world
- New: the modes share one world on this device. Your Player Career player, your Coach-mode coach and your Commissioner league's rules are noted as you play.
- In saves with "Shared world" on: your Coach-mode coach can turn up as a candidate when you hire (AD, Coach, NFL GM); your Player Career player can turn up on an AD recruiting board in his senior year of high school, and in an NFL draft class when his college years are up. Any save can adopt your Commissioner league's rules with one button in League rules.
- Only names, positions, ratings and teams are shared. Nothing already in a save changes, the coach you are playing never shows up in his own hiring pool, and saves from before this update keep the shared world off until you turn it on.

## 9.44 — Eras
- New: over a long save the sport changes around you. Every six to eight seasons a new era begins: a TV-money boom (more media money in college, a jump in the NFL salary cap), a bigger playoff, a realignment wave that pulls strong programs into the power conferences, or a rules change. Never the same kind twice in a row.
- In Commissioner mode you make those calls yourself, and your biggest ones (TV deals, labor deals, realignment) are recorded as the eras of your league.
- Eras are listed in League history and in the History hall. New saves have them on; saves from before this update keep their rules fixed until you turn "League eras" on in League rules.

## 9.43 — Prospect camps
- New: a summer camp on the recruiting page (AD and Coach). Once a season, invite up to eight recruits. They run drills in front of your staff: each gets a camp grade and a 40 time, you get an exact read on them from then on, and the trip to campus lifts their interest. The camp costs money.
- New (NFL GM): a prospect camp on the draft page. Once a draft season, invite up to six prospects from outside the top 64 for a full read, so late-round sleepers are easier to find.

## 9.42 — Share your league as a challenge
- New: on the Save file page (AD and NFL GM), "Share this league as a challenge" turns the league you're playing into a code: the same teams, rosters, money and history, with goals and a deadline you pick (checked the same way as a built challenge).

## 9.41 — Live games pick up where you left them
